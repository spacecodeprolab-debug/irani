An Iranian baker's cafe,
a family's kitchen.

Irani cafes came to India with immigrants from Iran who brought their tea-house and bakery culture. Ahmedabad's is one of the few still serving.

Colonial era

The first Irani cafes

Iranian immigrants bring tea-house and bakery culture to Indian cities.

Over 80 years ago

A baker opens his doors

Behram Irani, who came from Iran, founds the cafe near the old Shaukat Maidan, Bhadra and Lal Darwaja.

Until the 1960s

Bombay Bakery and Irani Hotel

That was its name then. The bakery section later closed because of nearby road extension work.

The freedom movement

A meeting place

Freedom fighters gathered here. The cafe was also known for giving free meals to nearby fakirs.

About seventy years ago

A local family takes over

Yusuf Patel, the third generation, runs it today.

Today

An old-city institution

Muslim-owned and operated, and known for Irani chai, bun maska and mutton keema.
